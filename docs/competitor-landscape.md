# Risto — competitor landscape

Drafted August 2026. Mix of live web research (dated findings cited inline) and general market knowledge. Treat the named-company lists as a map to verify locally, not a due-diligence document — private-company facts, especially acquisitions, go stale fast.

---

## How to read this

"Competitor" means five different things in this market, and they don't compete with each other. Risto sits across four of the five, which is unusual — and is the actual story.

| Category | What they sell | Examples | Threat to Risto |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. POS / till-led** | The cash register, extended | Toast, Lightspeed, Foodics, Tilby | Own the merchant relationship. Risto deliberately sits *next to* these |
| **2. Reservations & guest CRM** | The booking book | OpenTable, TheFork, SevenRooms, Zenchef | Compete directly for the front-of-house budget |
| **3. Back-office / ops** | Stock, labour, cost control | Nory, Restaurant365, MarginEdge, Mapal | Compete for the "one system" narrative |
| **4. AI voice / phone agents** | Answering the phone | Slang.ai, Loman, Hostie | **Compete directly with Risto's differentiator** |
| **5. Pay-at-table** | The QR bill | Sunday, Qlub, Yumminn, me&u | A feature elsewhere, a company here |

The genuinely new category is **6: AI-native restaurant operating systems** — one system covering the whole venue, built AI-first. That's Risto's category, it's about two years old, and it's funded.

---

## The four companies that matter most to you

**allO** (Munich) — the closest thing to a direct competitor anywhere. AI-native restaurant operating system, AI agents handling reservations, inventory and menu updates, 1,000+ locations in Germany. Raised a $14M / €12M Series A led by Zigg Capital in May 2026, explicitly to expand across Europe. Same thesis as Risto, two years ahead on capital, and heading for your market.

**Nory** (London / Dublin) — AI-native restaurant management: forecasting, labour, inventory, payroll. €31M Series B in September 2025, expanding to the US. Founded by a restaurant operator, which is the same credibility story you have. Weaker than Risto on service-floor and guest-facing; much stronger on back-office analytics.

**Zenchef** (Paris) — the European reservations consolidator. Absorbed Formitable (Netherlands) and Guestonline (France). Commission-free bookings, table management, CRM, prepayments, marketing. This is who an Italian, French, Spanish or Greek restaurateur is most likely to already be paying for the booking half of Risto.

**TheFork Manager** (Tripadvisor) — the incumbent in Italy and Spain. Not the best product, but it arrives free-ish with the marketplace that already sends the restaurant covers. It's the default your sales conversation has to displace, or coexist with.

## Region by region

### Italy — your first market

| Company | What it is |
|---|---|
| **TheFork Manager** (Tripadvisor) | Booking marketplace + manager tool. The default. Bundled with demand, which is the hard part to compete with |
| **Plateform** | Milan. Reservations + guest CRM + marketing. ~850 restaurants. Integrates with Tilby and Cassa in Cloud rather than replacing them |
| **Tilby** (Zucchetti) | Cloud POS for restaurants and bars. Part of Zucchetti, Italy's largest software group. Deep delivery and fiscal integrations |
| **Cassa in Cloud** (TeamSystem) | The other big-group cloud till. Electronic receipts, e-invoicing |
| **Passepartout Menù** | On-premise restaurant management, strong in independents. **You integrate with this rather than fight it — that's a real advantage** |
| **Scloby, Nexi SmartPOS, Ristoworks, Deliverart, YellGo, AlVolo** | Smaller POS, ordering and delivery tools |
| **Quandoo, OpenTable** | Foreign booking platforms with Italian presence |

**Read:** Italy is POS-heavy and reservations-light. The two giants (Zucchetti, TeamSystem) sell tills and accounting, not service-floor software. Nobody Italian is selling an AI phone host. That gap is real and it is yours to take — for now.

| **UK / Ireland** | **Nory**, Lightspeed, Zonal, Access Hospitality, ResDiary, SevenRooms, Flipdish, storekit, Tissl |

| **Nordics** | **Superb** (Copenhagen — "guest experience platform", closest positioning to Risto in the Nordics), Caspeco, Trivec, Onslip, DinnerBooking, Waiteraid |
| **Greece — your third market** | **i-host** (the Greek digital reservation book — reservations, tables, guest CRM, mobile companion app; the local incumbent to beat), BIM POS Hoster (Lebanese, sold across Greece and the Middle East), Dinemetrics, plus Softone/Entersoft ERPs on the back office and international POS |

**Read on Greece:** thin and fragmented. i-host owns the booking book, but there is no Greek all-in-one and no Greek AI voice agent. It's the easiest of your three expansion markets to enter on product merit — and the hardest on language, since the voice agent has to be genuinely good in Greek before it's a selling point rather than a liability.

### USA & Canada — the deepest and most crowded market

| Segment | Players |
|---|---|
| **POS / platform** | **Toast** (the gorilla — POS, payroll, marketing, payments, everything), Square for Restaurants, Lightspeed, Clover, TouchBistro, SpotOn, Revel, HungerRush, Oracle MICROS Simphony, NCR Aloha, PAR Brink |
| **Reservations & guest** | OpenTable, Resy (Amex), **SevenRooms**, Tock, Yelp Guest Manager, Wisely (Olo) |
| **Back office** | Restaurant365, MarginEdge, Craftable, Crunchtime, 7shifts (labour), Fourth |
| **Kitchen displays** | QSR Automations, Fresh KDS, Expo |
| **AI phone agents** | **Slang.ai, Loman.ai, Maple, Hostie.ai, Goodcall, Rosie, VoiceBit, RingFoods, ConverseNow, Presto, SoundHound** |
| **Canada** | TouchBistro, Lightspeed, Silverware, Veloce, Push Operations |

**The finding you most need to hear:** AI phone answering for restaurants is now a crowded, funded category in the US, with at least ten named vendors. Slang.ai charges **$399–599 per location per month** and Loman **$299/month for 500 minutes** — for the phone agent *alone*, with no floor plan, no orders, no kitchen, no HACCP.

Two implications. First, don't ever pitch the voice agent as a world-first — an American investor or an informed operator will know better in a second. Second, and much more usefully: those prices are your pricing anchor. Risto bundles that capability into a system that does everything else too. In Europe that comparison is still fresh.

| **Qlub** (UAE) | Pay-at-table by QR — the same feature you built, sold as a company |

**Read:** the highest-spending, most tech-forward market on the list, and the most defended. Foodics is very hard to displace on POS. But Arabic-language AI phone answering during Ramadan hours is a real, unsolved, high-value problem — if you ever go here, go with the voice agent first, not the whole suite.

## What this means for Risto

**Nobody in your segment is doing all of it.** POS vendors don't do reservations properly. Reservation platforms don't do kitchen displays. Back-office platforms don't touch the service floor. The voice agents do one phone call and stop. Risto covering bookings → floor → orders → kitchen → payment → stock → HACCP → staff in one live system is genuinely uncommon, and that — not the AI — is the sentence to lead with.

**Your differentiator is narrower than it feels, and wider than you'd think.** The AI voice agent is a solved, priced, crowded product in the US. In Italy it is not. But the durable version of the claim isn't "we have AI on the phone" — it's *"our AI books against the same live availability the floor plan uses"*. Standalone phone agents can't do that, because they don't own the floor plan. That's a structural advantage no amount of their funding erases.

**The till integration is your wedge, not a footnote.** Every competitor in Italy asks a restaurateur to change their cash register. That's the objection that kills deals. Risto sitting beside Passepartout and closing the order in it is the single most sellable fact on this page.

**Price above what a phone agent costs alone.** Slang and Loman get $300–600 a month for answering the phone. That's your ceiling reference and it's higher than most European restaurant software.

**The thing to watch is allO.** German, funded in May 2026 specifically to expand across Europe, same category, same AI-native pitch. Italy is an obvious market for them. You have a real head start on depth — HACCP, banquets, till integration, the floor detail — and they have twelve million euros. Depth is defensible for maybe eighteen months.
