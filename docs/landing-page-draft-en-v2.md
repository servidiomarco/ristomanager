# Risto — landing page, draft 2

Reworked against the competitor research in `competitor-landscape.md`. Draft 1 (`landing-page-draft-en.md`) stays as it is — the full 18-module feature inventory and the role-by-role value table live there and are still current. This file replaces only the **page copy and positioning**.

What changed and why:

1. **"Keep your till" moved from a footnote to the second thing on the page.** Every competitor in Italy asks a restaurateur to replace their cash register. That objection kills deals before features matter.
2. **The AI claim rewritten.** Ten funded vendors sell restaurant phone answering. "We have AI on the phone" is not a claim — "it books against the same live availability my floor plan uses" is, and no standalone agent can copy it.
3. **A comparison section added.** Buyers arrive already paying TheFork or Plateform. The page has to answer "why not just keep what I have" explicitly rather than hoping.
4. **Plans reanchored.** Still no numbers, but framed against what a phone-answering service costs on its own.
5. **The whole page tightened.** Draft 1 had six feature blocks of three paragraphs each. Nobody reads that.

---

# Positioning

**The sentence the whole page defends:**

> Risto is the one system your restaurant runs its service on — and it works with the till you already have.

**The proof that makes it credible:** it runs a real restaurant every day.

**The differentiator that survives a competitor reading it:** Risto's AI answers the phone and books against the *same live availability the floor plan uses*. A standalone phone agent can't, because it doesn't own the floor plan. That's structural, not a feature.

**What we don't claim:** first, only, revolutionary, percentages, number of customers.

## Feature blocks

Five, not six. Each one is a heading, three lines, one screenshot. Cut anything that doesn't survive being read at a bus stop.

---

### The phone answers itself — and it knows what's actually free

> An AI host takes your calls and books the table. It reads the same live availability your floor plan uses — your hours, your closures, the tables genuinely free tonight — so it never gives away a table you don't have.
>
> That's the part a standalone answering service can't do: it doesn't know your room. Every call is recorded and transcribed, missed calls become follow-ups, and you can switch it off whenever you want humans answering.

*Small:* Answering in Italian today. English, Spanish and Greek in progress.

*Note: never write "the first" or "the only". Ten companies sell restaurant phone agents. None of them own the floor plan — that's the whole claim, and it's enough.*

---

### Every booking channel, one book

> Phone, WhatsApp, SMS, email, your Google profile, walk-ins. One list, each booking tagged with where it came from, so you can see which channel actually fills the room.
>
> Bookings move on their own — confirmed becomes arriving twenty minutes out, seated becomes leaving when a table runs long. Guest allergies and preferences attach themselves. Take a deposit on web bookings and no-shows stop being free.

---

### A floor plan that matches tonight's room

> Merge two tables for a party of nine, hide the terrace when it rains, close a room for lunch — tonight only. Tomorrow it's back on its own.
>
> Table colour is the state, and it's the same colour everywhere in the system. Double-booking the same table is refused, not discovered at eight o'clock.

---

### Orders that reach the right station instantly

> Waiters order at the table, one-handed, on the phone in their pocket. Each kitchen station sees only its own queue, in type readable from a metre away.
>
> Courses are staggered so the grill and the pasta hit the pass together, and the expediter sees the whole table at once — the view that stops three stations working blind to each other. A plate sitting too long under the lamp starts flashing.

---

### Paid before you reach the table

> The guest scans, sees their bill on their own phone, pays. No app, no login. Split by item, by share or by amount.
>
> Card payments through Revolut or SumUp, payment links by WhatsApp for deposits and events — and the order closes in your existing till.

---

### The parts nobody enjoys

> **HACCP** — temperatures, oil, cleaning, goods-in, production. The five records an inspector asks for, filled in on a phone, printed as a report when it matters.
> **Stock** — products, locations and movements across kitchen, floor and bar.
> **Purchasing** — a shared list the team adds to during service, grouped by supplier so it splits into the calls you have to make.
> **Staff** — shifts per service, attendance, holidays, sick leave, contract dates. In the system, not in a group chat.

## Comparison

**Heading:** "I already pay for something."

Most visitors do. Answer it on the page rather than on the call.

| You currently use | What it gives you | What it doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| **A booking platform** (TheFork, Plateform, CoverManager, OpenTable) | Bookings and covers | Nothing after the guest sits down. No orders, no kitchen, no bill, no stock, no HACCP |
| **A cloud till** (Tilby, Cassa in Cloud, Toast) | The sale, the receipt, the accounting | The service itself. No arrivals, no course timing, no expediter view, no phone answering |
| **A phone answering service** (Slang, Loman) | Calls answered | It doesn't know your floor plan. It can't see the merged nine-top or tonight's closed room |
| **A back-office platform** (Nory, Restaurant365) | Forecasting, labour, food cost | Nothing your waiter or chef touches during service |
| **Paper and WhatsApp** | It's free | Everything above |

> Risto is the layer none of them cover: the two hours between the guest walking in and the bill being paid. It keeps your till, and it can replace your booking platform or sit next to it.

*Careful: name competitors by category, not by logo, if you'd rather not invite a direct comparison page. The version above names them because Italian restaurateurs already do, out loud, on the first call.*

## FAQ

**Do I have to replace my till?**
No. Risto works alongside your existing system and closes the order in it when a guest pays by QR.

**I already use TheFork. Do I drop it?**
Only if you want to. TheFork sends you covers; Risto runs what happens after they arrive. Plenty of restaurants keep both.

**What hardware do we need?**
The phones and tablets you already have. A screen in the kitchen helps — any TV with a browser.

**How long is setup?**
We build your rooms, tables and menu with you and train your team. Most venues run within a week.

**What if the internet drops?**
Orders and changes queue on the device and send when it returns. Service doesn't stop.

**Does the AI host sound like a robot?**
On the demo call we'll ring the restaurant line and you can listen.

**Where does our guest data live?**
It stays yours. Consent recorded with a timestamp, privacy notice included, and staff only see what their role allows.

**Which languages?**
Italian and English today. Spanish and Greek in progress.

# Notes

**Rules kept from draft 1.** Never set anything in capitals — word shapes vanish, and accented capitals degrade in Italian, Spanish and Greek alike. Say each thing once. No reassurance sentences.

**Build order for the page.** Hero video → keep-your-till → comparison table → demo form. Those four do the selling; the feature blocks are for the visitor who's already convinced and looking for a reason to say yes.

**What to build before this goes live.**
1. The 60-second service recording. Nothing else on the page works as hard.
2. Multi-tenancy, or an honest label on that column.
3. The Italian version — it's the market you'll actually sell to on day one, and the English page is for investors and the second year.

**What to watch.** allO (Munich) raised €12M in May 2026 for the same category and is expanding across Europe. Your defence is depth they don't have — HACCP, banquets, till integration, the floor detail — and depth stays defensible for maybe eighteen months. Ship the site.
